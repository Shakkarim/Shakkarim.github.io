Bio

Atmospheric scientist working at the intersection of numerical modeling, observations, and operational severe-weather forecasting.

About me

I am an NWP Research Scientist at the National Wind Institute (NWI), Texas Tech University, where I develop and maintain the Texas Ensemble Prediction System (TEPS): a 100-member, 2-km convection-allowing WRF ensemble that produces 48-hour severe-weather forecasts for Texas. I lead its transition to real-time operations, built its forecast products and product website, and present those products to NWS forecasters, emergency managers, and partner institutions.

Before joining NWI in 2026, I was a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the Severe Weather Research Center (SWRC), Hampton University, where I operated a satellite-assimilated severe-weather forecasting system, developed a multi-Doppler radar wind retrieval algorithm, ran large-eddy simulations of convection, and operated the Skyler-II phased-array radar. Earlier, at NC A&T State University, I studied urban impacts on convection over Chicago, wildfire-conducive mesoscale environments, African easterly waves, and orographic rainfall during typhoon passage over Taiwan.

I earned my Ph.D. in Atmospheric Science from NC A&T State University in 2021, and my M.S. and B.Sc. (Hons.) in Physics from the University of Chittagong, Bangladesh.
