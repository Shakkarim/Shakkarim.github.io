Welcome!

Hi there! I am an atmospheric scientist with 9+ years of experience running, analyzing, and validating numerical weather prediction (NWP) simulations. My research spans multiscale atmospheric processes, including convection, boundary-layer turbulence, urban–atmosphere interaction, wildfire-conducive environments, orographic effects, and tropical cyclone and tropical wave dynamics.

Current focus: Texas Ensemble Prediction System (TEPS) At the National Wind Institute I develop and maintain TEPS, a 100-member, 2-km convection-allowing WRF ensemble that produces 48-hour severe-weather forecasts for Texas, and I lead its move from a validated retrospective system into real-time operations.

My current and recent work includes:

  • Convection-allowing ensemble design, operations, and probabilistic forecast products
  • Doppler radar wind retrieval and LES-based radar simulator development
  • Satellite-assimilated severe-weather forecasting
  • Tropical cyclone–urban interaction
  • Large-eddy simulation of convection and turbulence

To understand complex, multiscale weather and support real-time forecasting, I combine high-resolution numerical models with atmospheric observations and radar and satellite remote sensing, and I work closely with NWS forecasters and emergency managers on how forecast products are used.
